ECON 5360 Tutorial Introduction#

DING Minjie, Spring 2025#

This is tutorial notes for ECON 5360 Monetary Economics in Spring 2025, prepared by teaching assistant DING Minjie.

This year, a large proportion of students from Master of Economics program choose Research Preparation Concentration. Familiarity with modeling and coding in monetary economics is important for students from research track. So, apart from regualr explanation of homework and exam, the tutorial section also covers this note, hope it will help you.

I have a background solely in economics, with limited mathematics and only high school physics. So, it takes me a long time to learn macroeconomic models. Also, I cannot handle overly complex models. Therefore, my note is relatively simple, and I’ve tried to add comments to most lines of code to make it readable. And there must be errors due to my immature understanding, please contact me at mdingaa@connect.ust.hk for errors.

MATLAB is primary language here. I also use Python and Dynare in some cases.

The notes seek help from many professors’ personal websites and course materials. I am very grateful to them.

Coding Principles#

This notes build heavily on coding, so here are some of my programming principles:

Variable names:

  1. When there are more than one elements in variable name, I use _ to join them.

    • “a_max” means “maximum of a”. I will not use “amax”, since I sometimes forget its meaning and may take it as “I am ax”.

  1. Try to avoid use one letter as abbreviation. Use it only for important variables.

    • In consumption saving problem, a is for asset, c is for consumption. Most other variable won’t have a one-letter name.

  1. Try to use full word, in order to eliminate confusion. As long as I can’t get clear meaning of an abbreviation outside the script, I use full word. If I can get the correct meaning by google, I accept the abbreviation.

    • When introduce tolerance, “tolerance” is used instead of “tole”, since I can’t link “tole” to tolerance after several days. And when I google “tole”, there is also link to “tolerance”.

    • When I google “diff”, I can know usually it means “difference”, so “diff” is accepted.

  1. Use the uppercase for vector and matrix.

    • In consumption saving problem, A is for asset vector, V is for value function vector.

  1. When there are several variables under one class, put their class name in advance.

    • for a series of iteration variable, I use “iteration_max” to denote max times of iteration, instead of “max_iteration”.

Space:

  1. Use a space between operation and numbers.

    • “1 + 1 = 2” is used, instead of “1+1=2”.

    • “a = [1, 2, 3]” is used, instead of “a = [1,2,3]”.
